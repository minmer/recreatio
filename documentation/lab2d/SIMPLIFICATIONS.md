# 2D lab engine (v2) — simplifications

Every approximation of the model, with why it is there and what it changes. The "About this model" panel shows the
same list to students (Polish first, two levels of wording, `i18n.ts` → `SIMPLIFICATIONS_PL`) — that models simplify
reality is itself a lesson. When a milestone adds or removes an approximation, this file changes with the code.
Architecture: [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md). Numbers: [MILESTONES.md](MILESTONES.md).

| # | Simplification | Why | What it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A 2D slab.** The box is a slab of depth D (1 cm); nothing moves across it, and the front and back faces neither lose heat nor touch anything. | 3D at this resolution is beyond a school laptop in real time. | Flows are planar. Masses and energies are real for the slab. |
| 2 | **Fields on cells of 1–3 mm.** Temperature, pressure, gas velocity, composition and fog are one value per cell. | The cell count decides the speed. | Anything thinner than a cell (the thermal boundary layer at a hot wall, a thin film) is not resolved; the wall contact temperature (#9) stands in for the boundary layer. |
| 3 | **Separate liquid and gas solves.** The liquid sees the gas as a uniform pressure; the gas sees the liquid as a moving wall. | Water is about 830 times denser than air; one-way coupling of pressure is accurate for that ratio and far cheaper. | Air trapped in water, or air blown hard against a water surface, is not modelled (MultiFLIP is the upgrade path). |
| 4 | **Low-Mach gas.** Gas density follows temperature and composition at the region's pressure p0; local pressure differences only drive flow. | The standard model for slow, hot flows; it keeps the step large. | No sound, no pressure waves; explosions are out of reach (a later milestone needs a compressible solver). |
| 5 | **Constant-density liquid projection with a temperature-dependent body force.** | Exact hydrostatics with real densities, without the cost and instability of a variable-density projection. | Buoyancy of warm water is right; the liquid's pressure waves are not modelled (water is incompressible here; its pressure work V·Δp0 in a sealed box is booked). |
| 6 | **No surface tension on the resolved water surface.** | The capillary length of water (≈ 2.7 mm) is about one cell; resolving it needs a much smaller time step. Gravity therefore stays at 0.25 g or more. | Droplets and meniscuses on the cell scale take no shape from surface tension; zero gravity is not available in M1. Surface tension acts through the bubble models (pressure inside a bubble, nucleation, departure). |
| 7 | **No radiation.** Heat moves by conduction, convection and phase change only. | Radiation matters at a glowing plate, not in a boiling beaker. | A very hot plate loses less heat than in reality. It is drawn redder above 60 °C as a hint, not as computed glow. |
| 8 | **Heat loss through one coefficient** h_ext = 10 W/(m²·K) on the box walls, in series with half a cell. | The room outside is not simulated. | Cooling is approximate; the front and back of the slab lose nothing (#1). |
| 9 | **Wall contact temperature.** Where liquid touches a solid, nucleation and attached bubbles use T_f = (k_s T_s + k_l T_l)/(k_s + k_l). | Cells of 2 mm cannot resolve the thermal boundary layer; without this the wall overheats by tens of kelvin. | Superheat at the wall is approximate; nucleation onsets follow the cavity model, not a resolved boundary layer. |
| 10 | **Cavities as a sampled list per surface face.** At most eight per solid face, mouth radii from a truncated power law per material (steel calibrated to Lemmert & Chawla at 1 atm; glass at most 3 µm, boiling chips up to 150 µm). | Cavities are microscopic. | Where and when bubbles form is statistically right for the material, not for a particular real surface. Moving a solid redraws its cavities. |
| 11 | **Bubbles with correlations.** Growth (Plesset–Zwick, Ranz–Marshall), departure (Fritz), drag (Tomiyama, contaminated water); no lift, no bubble collisions except coalescence. | Bubbles are smaller than or comparable to a cell; their flow cannot be resolved. | Bubble sizes, growth and rise speeds follow the correlations within their accuracy (about 10–20 %); bubble shapes are not drawn deformed. |
| 12 | **Ideal gases**, dry air = N₂ + O₂, vapour enthalpy depending on temperature only; gas properties read from 1 K tables. | Accurate within a fraction of a per cent at these pressures. | The latent heat away from 100 °C follows Kirchhoff's law from the IF97 value at 100 °C (0.5 % low at 20 °C). |
| 13 | **Fog is a field, not droplets.** Condensed water in the gas is a mass per cell that moves with the gas, without falling. | Fog droplets are microscopic. | Fog does not settle or rain out; dew forms only on walls. |
| 14 | **The room beyond an open side is ambient air.** Inflow brings ambient air; what leaves is booked as escaped. | The room is not simulated. | Steam leaving the box is counted, not followed. |
| 15 | **Apparatus is static and rigid.** Beakers and plates are solid cells. The drag tool moves them instantly, by whole cells, with the water and bubbles inside (the air stays). | Movable and melting solids come in later milestones. | A beaker cannot be knocked over or moved by the flow; a dragged beaker does not slosh. |
| 16 | **Free-slip walls for the liquid.** At the box walls the APIC stencils are completed by mirrored ghost nodes; solids push particles out along their distance field. | A no-slip boundary layer is far thinner than a cell; truncated stencils also made water climb the walls. | Water slides along walls without friction; near-wall flows are slightly too fast. |
| 17 | **No gas pockets in the water except bubbles.** A cell may turn into gas only if it was gas, touches gas or touches an open side. | A void inside the liquid would have no pressure law in this model (like a liquid region without a free surface, which the tools refuse). | No cavitation; air entrained by splashing closes up instead of forming pockets. |
| 18 | **Many small bubbles at a hot wall.** One wall cell holds the whole local wall superheat, so a cavity is ready again as soon as its cell is warm. | The thin superheated layer that really limits re-nucleation is not resolved (#2, #9). | In subcooled water thousands of tiny bubbles per second form and collapse at the bottom (energy is right; the count is too high). |
| 19 | **Bubbles inside the slab.** Spheres until they are as wide as the slab, then flattened, then discs of depth D; the Laplace pressure stays 2σ/r for discs. Bubbles below 20 µm move with the water. | The slab has a depth; a disc's real meniscus adds about 2σ/D (15 Pa at 1 cm). | Large bubbles are slightly too easy to compress (15 Pa of about 100 kPa); tiny bubbles do not slip. |
| 20 | **Bursts spread over the gas above.** A bursting bubble gives its content to as many gas cells above it as its volume needs; the gas volume correction is capped at ±½ cell per step. | A large bubble bursting into one small cell would demand a velocity the transport cannot carry. | A big burst is pushed out over a few steps (a short overpressure) instead of as an instant jet. |
| 21 | **Fog opacity is a drawing assumption.** White with opacity from Beer–Lambert for droplets of 1.5 µm radius seen through 7 cm (a beaker's width), not through the 1 cm slab. | Through 1 cm real steam fog would be almost invisible. | The fog mass is computed; how white it looks is an assumption (with 1 µm droplets it would look denser, with 5 µm thinner). |

## Performance-related choices

These do not change the physics model, but they decide what a student's computer can show (MILESTONES.md):

- dt = min(1/120 s, 0.9 Δx/u_max): real time needs two steps per 60 Hz frame. A slower computer runs the same
  simulation in slow motion; the shell says so and offers a coarser cell.
- Pressure solves stop at a relative residual of 1e-5 (the prompt's real-time tolerance); the bookkeeping of mass and
  energy is exact regardless of solver tolerances.
